How the Ingredient Ratio Calculator Works

The Ingredient Ratio Calculator applies standardised culinary conversion factors and cooking science principles to your inputs. Conversion values are sourced from internationally recognised culinary standards, and ingredient-specific adjustments are made where volume-to-weight ratios differ significantly between ingredients.

For baking especially, weight-based measurements (grams) are more accurate than volume (cups, tablespoons) because ingredient density varies. This calculator supports both approaches and converts between them where needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate are the conversions and calculations?

All conversions use standardised culinary measurement values (US customary, metric, and UK imperial). Ingredient-specific calculations account for the fact that volume-to-weight ratios differ significantly between ingredients — a cup of flour weighs very differently from a cup of sugar.

Why do cooking measurements differ between countries?

The US uses customary units (cups, tablespoons, Fahrenheit) while most of the world uses metric (grams, millilitres, Celsius). UK recipes historically use a mix, and Australian tablespoons differ from US tablespoons. This calculator handles all major regional standards.

Can I use these calculations for baking?

Baking is particularly sensitive to precise measurements. Weight-based measurements (grams) are recommended over volume for baking because they are more accurate and repeatable. The calculator supports weight conversions specifically to help with precision baking.

How do I scale a recipe up or down?

For most recipes, scaling ingredients linearly works well. However, cooking times, temperatures, and leavening agents do not always scale linearly — a recipe doubled in volume may take only 25% longer to cook, not twice as long. The calculator addresses scaling factors where they apply.

Are the nutritional estimates accurate for recipes?

Nutritional estimates are based on standard USDA food composition data. Actual values vary by brand, preparation method, and cooking technique. Use the outputs as reliable guides for general dietary planning rather than clinical-grade precision.

Baker's Percentages

In baking, ingredients are expressed as a percentage of flour weight (100%). This makes scaling easy and helps understand dough hydration. A 65% hydration bread means 65g of water per 100g of flour.
